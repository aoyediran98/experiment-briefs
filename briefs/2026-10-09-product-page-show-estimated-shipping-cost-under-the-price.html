1 · The why

Problem or opportunity

GA4 shows 41% of users who reach the shipping step abandon checkout. The top support theme last month was 'shipping cost wasn't shown earlier'. Today the first place a user sees any shipping cost is the shipping step, after they have already entered the checkout.

Hypothesis

Because 41% of users who reach the shipping step abandon checkout and the top support theme is that shipping cost was not shown earlier, showing an estimated shipping cost under the price on the product page will increase checkout completion rate, because users who continue to checkout will already have accepted the full cost and will not be surprised at the shipping step.

The decision this informs

  • Win: Ship the shipping estimate to 100% of product pages, then test showing it in the cart and on listing pages.
  • Loss: Keep the product page as it is. Next test the level of the shipping cost itself (a free-shipping threshold), since seeing the cost earlier put users off rather than reassured them.
  • Flat: Conclude that when the cost is shown is not the driver of shipping-step abandonment. Do not iterate on placement; investigate the shipping step itself (delivery options, speed, form friction).

Prior evidence

  • No previous test on shipping cost visibility is known. Confirm against the test log before launch.

2 · What is being tested

Control

Current product page: price shown with no shipping cost or estimate. Shipping cost first appears at the shipping step of checkout.

Variants

Variant B
Add one line directly under the price showing the estimated shipping cost for the user's region. Nothing else on the product page or in checkout changes.
Estimated shipping: [cost for the user's region]
Final shipping is confirmed at checkout.
Moves the shipping cost to the point where the buying decision is made, so it is part of the price the user accepts rather than a surprise three steps later.

Audience and targeting

Eligible: All users who view a product page, any device, any traffic source.

Excluded: Internal IPs, and users whose shipping region cannot be estimated (they see control and are not assigned). List any concurrent checkout or pricing tests here and exclude their participants before launch.

Entry point: Assignment happens on the first product page view, since that is where the change is shown.

Unit of randomisation

User (first-party ID, sticky across sessions). A purchase often spans several sessions, so session-level assignment would let one person see the product page both with and without the estimate.

3 · How it will be judged

Primary metric

Checkout completion rate

Numerator: Assigned users who complete an order
Denominator: Users assigned on first product page view
Attribution window: 7 days from assignment

Pre-specified success criteria

At the pre-registered end of week 3, Variant B is a win if checkout completion is at least 3.41% (10% relative on the 3.1% baseline) with p < 0.05 two-sided and all three guardrails hold. It is a loss if completion is significantly lower, or if the add-to-cart or revenue guardrail is breached regardless of the completion result. Anything else is inconclusive and the test is not extended.

Secondary metrics

  • Abandonment rate at the shipping step (currently 41%)
  • Add-to-cart rate from the product page
  • Share of support contacts mentioning shipping cost

Guardrail metrics

  • Add-to-cart rate must not fall by more than 5% relative (the estimate must not put off more users than it reassures)
  • Revenue per assigned user must not fall
  • Product page load time must not rise by more than 200 ms at the 75th percentile

Why this MDE

A 10% relative lift on 3.1% completion is about 124 extra orders a week at full rollout on 40,000 weekly sessions. Multiply by average order value to put it in money. A smaller lift would be hard to separate from weekly noise at this traffic and would not clearly justify maintaining region-level shipping estimates on every product page.

4 · Statistics and logistics

Baseline

3.10% for the primary metric. Source: GA4, checkout completion among product page viewers, as supplied (3.1%). Date range and exact audience definition to be confirmed against the test audience before launch.

Sample size and duration

51,438 per arm (102,876 total) at 40,000 eligible units a week. Run for 3 full weeks at α=0.05, power 80%. Fixed before launch.

Traffic allocation

50/50 from launch, no ramp.

Stopping rule

Run the full pre-registered 3 weeks and read once at the end. No interim significance reads. The only early stop is an add-to-cart or revenue guardrail breach of more than 10% relative confirmed over 3 consecutive days, or a tracking fault.

Analysis plan: pre-registered segments

  • Mobile vs desktop
  • New vs returning users
  • Shipping region or cost band (low vs high estimated shipping)

5 · Risks and ownership

Risks and dependencies

  • Traffic is counted in sessions but assignment is by user. Unique users will be fewer than 40,000 a week, so confirm weekly unique product page users before launch; if materially lower, the run time grows.
  • The estimate must match what checkout charges. A wrong estimate creates a second surprise and would bias the result against the variant.
  • Promotions, free-shipping offers or paid campaigns during the run change how much the shipping cost matters; record any that overlap.
  • Overlap with other tests on the product page or checkout; exclude their participants.

QA and tracking checks before launch

  • Assignment event fires once per user and matches the arm shown (checked on iOS Safari, Android Chrome, desktop Chrome)
  • The estimate shown equals the shipping charged at checkout for a sample of regions and basket sizes
  • Order event fires for both arms and joins to the assigned user ID
  • Sample ratio check at 48 hours: arms within 1% of 50/50 or the test is paused

Owners and sign-off

How the numbers were calculated

Sample size per arm uses the two-proportion z-test: n = (z1−α/2·√(2p̄(1−p̄)) + z1−β·√(p₁(1−p₁)+p₂(1−p₂)))² / (p₂−p₁)², with p₂ = p₁·(1+MDE). Run time is total sample over weekly eligible units, rounded up, with a two-week floor. These figures were fixed before the brief was written and the brief was generated around them, so the success criteria are pre-registered rather than fitted afterwards.
